## 2. Top Equestrian-Active Countries by Industry Size

### United States — the dominant market
- **Economic impact:** USD 122 B total economic impact (AHCF / American Horse Council Foundation, 2023 Economic Impact Study; up from USD 102 B in 2005).
- **Horse population:** ~6.7 M (AHCF 2023), with USEF citing 9.2 M when including all uses.
- **Participation:** ~4.6 M Americans involved in the industry (USEF).
- **Equipment revenue:** USD 3.5 B+ in 2024 (Equerry / Co, 2025).
- **Employment:** 1.74 M total jobs; USD 79 B in wages.

### Germany — Europe's heart
- **Horses:** ~1.25 M privately owned (FN / German Equestrian Federation projections, 2023). Largest horse population in Europe.
- **Riders:** 2.32 M self-identified riders; 840 K ride regularly; 11.2 M express interest in horses (FN survey).
- **Clubs / facilities:** 7,600 riding clubs + 4,000 commercial centres; 42,000 horse-keeping farms.
- **FN members:** 709,000.
- **Breeding:** 25 breed associations, 55,600 brood mares, 3,500 sires.

### United Kingdom
- **Market value:** ~£5 B / USD ~6.3 B (BETA / British Equestrian Trade Association, National Equestrian Survey 2022-23). Some industry voices cite USD 9.8 B when including racing.
- **Riders:** 1.82 M ride monthly, 3.2 M ride at least once a year.
- **Horse-keeping households:** 331,000.

### France
- **FFE members:** 675,186 in 2023 (rebounding from a 2012-2020 decline).
- **Total riders:** ~3 M occasional + regular (FFE).
- **Riding schools:** 8,000+ ; FFE National Equestrian Tourism Committee covers 2,500 establishments.
- **Employment:** ~45,000.
- **Demography:** 85 % female; 61 % aged ≤18; 80 % of licences are leisure (FFE 2023).

### Netherlands — export powerhouse
- **Horses:** ~450,000; ~500,000 riders; 80 % female (Spoga Horse / FEI).
- **Industry turnover:** EUR 1.5 B / yr; 6,600 affiliated companies.
- **KNHS members:** 200,000 (50,000 competitive); ~765,000 annual competition entries.
- **KWPN breeding:** ~10,000 foals / yr. The Netherlands is the **world's largest horse-export country** (EUR 200 M of horses exported in 2013; structurally higher today).

### Belgium
- **Horses:** ~400,000 — **highest horse density in Europe** (Belga News, 2024).
- **Riders:** ~200,000 active.
- **Industry turnover (Flanders alone):** EUR 1.43 B; EUR 200 M annual horse exports.
- **Breeding strength:** ~30 % of horses at the Paris 2024 Olympics were Belgian-bred.

### Sweden
- **Horses:** ~355,000 (one of Europe's most horse-dense per capita).
- **Riders:** 500 K regular + 500 K in regular horse contact (HNS / National Horse Industry Foundation).
- **Economic impact:** SEK 32 B turnover; **0.5 % of Swedish GDP**.
- Equestrianism is the 2nd-largest youth sport (after football), girl-dominated.

### Ireland
- **Sport horse population:** ~136,000 (racehorses excluded).
- **Sector turnover:** EUR 816 M; 14,000 jobs.
- One of Europe's iconic equestrian tourism destinations.

### Australia
- **Horses:** 1.03 M (excl. racehorses + ~400 K feral).
- **Equine economic activity (non-racing):** AUD 15.7 B; 100,000+ FTE jobs (Australian Horse Industry Council / IER, March 2025).
- **Equestrian Australia members:** 22,779 (2023); 814 coaches, 1,129 officials.

### Argentina — polo capital
- The only country producing the dedicated **Polo Argentino** pony.
- ~3,000 registered polo players nationally + a much larger informal base.
- **Largest polo-pony exporter in the world.** 1924 + 1936 Olympic polo gold.

### Brazil
- 482 registered athletes, 607 horses, 103 events (CBH / Confederação Brasileira de Hipismo, FEI data 2025) — though the broader gaucho / leisure rider base is much larger across São Paulo, Paraná and Rio Grande do Sul.

## 4. Emerging Markets — Fastest Growth

### China — the boom case
- Equestrian clubs: **20 in 2011 → 2,000 in 2024** (FEI President, Global Times Aug 2024). One source cites 907 → 2,815 (2016-2024).
- **2,160 registered clubs and 682,000 riders** by 2019 (Pegasus / China Daily).
- **1.19 M consumers across 2,000 clubs** in 2019, +20 % in 2 years (China Daily, 2022).
- Shanghai now hosts the FEI Global Champions Tour and built a permanent equestrian venue in 2024.
- Caveat: post-2021 the boom has cooled; consolidation has begun, but FEI still calls China "the largest growth potential market" globally.

### Saudi Arabia & UAE
- **The Saudi Cup** is the world's richest horse race — USD 35 M+ purse (SEA.gov.sa); 1,400+ entries from 22 countries in 2023.
- 22 riding clubs and equestrian establishments in Saudi Arabia.
- Notable shift: young Saudi women now training as jockeys; King Abdulaziz Equestrian Center enrolment rising.
- UAE-Saudi-Bahrain talks on a coordinated Gulf horseracing calendar (Arabian Business).
- Equerry / Co projects the Middle East to "solidify status as a global equine powerhouse through 2032."

### Brazil & Latin America
- Gaucho riding tradition + growing competitive scene (São Paulo, Paraná, Rio Grande do Sul, DF).
- Polo expanding regionally — "Brazil, Chile, Uruguay growing significantly in polo landscapes" (FEI).

### Southeast Asia
- Thailand specifically called out as an "emerging focal point" (Equerry / Co, 2025).
- Asia-Pacific tourism CAGR 8.4 % is driven by Thailand, Mongolia, Bhutan, New Zealand alongside China & India.

### Mexico
- Strong charrería tradition (working horse sport). Less data in international research reports — likely a USD 200–500 M equipment market based on regional GDP proxy.

## 5. India Deep-Dive

### Hard data we found
- **Equestrian Federation of India (EFI)** has over 1,030 members and 155 private-club electoral-college members (EFI / Indian Kanoon court doc, 2023).
- **Indian Polo Association (IPA)** has 35 affiliate clubs; founded 1892; most activity routed through the **61 Cavalry** of the Indian Army.
- India is the **birthplace of modern polo** (first club: Silchar, Assam, 1834).
- **Markwide / FMI:** India horse riding equipment market projected to grow at **~8.4 % CAGR through 2035** — among the fastest of any country.

### Key clubs / facilities (founder's home turf)
- **Embassy International Riding School (EIRS), Bangalore** — 25+ years, Olympic-level coaching, hosts the Equestrian Premier League.
- **Amateur Riders' Club (ARC), Mumbai** — founded 1942, one of India's oldest civilian clubs.
- **Rambagh Polo Club, Jaipur** — Maharaja-era polo heritage.
- **Calcutta Polo Club** — the world's oldest still-existing polo club.
- **Delhi Riding Club, ASC Centre Bangalore, President's Bodyguard Delhi, Madras Race Club, Hyderabad Race Club** — anchor institutions.

### Market-size estimate (founder's reasoning)
There is no published India equestrian equipment market figure. Triangulating:

1. India's horse riding equipment CAGR is 8.4 % (FMI 2025). Working backwards from India's likely share of Asia-Pacific tack (~5-8 %, given that APAC is ~USD 200-250 M of the global USD 0.93-1.6 B):
   → India tack-only market estimated at **USD 12-20 M today**, climbing to **USD 25-40 M by 2030**.
2. Adding apparel, boots, helmets, feed/supplements, and stable supplies (broader "equestrian wallet"): apply the global ratio of full equipment-market : tack market (~6-7x).
   → **Total India equestrian goods market: USD 75-140 M today**, plausibly **USD 150-250 M by 2030**.
3. Add services (lessons, boarding, polo, breeding, tourism) — likely **3-5x the goods market** based on global benchmarks (UK is goods ~£0.5 B vs. industry ~£5 B; ~10x).
   → **Total India equestrian economy: USD 300-700 M today** (a wide range reflecting limited data).

EFI member growth, the rise of equestrian academies in Bangalore / Pune / Gurugram, and Embassy's Equestrian Premier League all point to **double-digit participation growth** in the urban, affluent segment that matters most for a marketplace.

### Drivers in India
- **Rising HNI / UHNI base** — Knight Frank India Wealth Report consistently shows 10-12 %/yr growth in USD-millionaire households; equestrian is squarely a luxury-aspirational category.
- **Women's participation** — globally 80–85 % female; the same pattern is emerging in Indian urban riding schools (e.g. ARC Mumbai, EIRS Bangalore membership skews young + female).
- **School-affiliated programmes** in Bangalore, Pune, Delhi-NCR, Mumbai.
- **Polo tourism** in Rajasthan + Manipur Sangai Festival visibility.
- **Cultural lineage** — India is the birthplace of modern polo; this is a story investors will recognise.

### Headwinds in India
- **Fragmented supply chain** — most premium tack is imported (UK, Germany, Argentina), with no organised domestic distribution. (This is precisely the gap Indusequine fills.)
- **Stable infrastructure cost** — urban land scarcity makes new clubs hard.
- **EFI vs. IPA governance split** — polo and equestrian sport sit under different bodies, complicating umbrella growth narratives.
- **Animal welfare scrutiny** — rising concern, especially around urban carriage horses and unregistered stables.
- **Low base** — total addressable market today is small in absolute terms, even if growth rates are high.

## 6. Drivers & Headwinds by Region

| Region | Top growth drivers | Top headwinds |
|---|---|---|
| **North America** | Wellness positioning, women's participation, e-commerce, premium pet-style spend | Rising feed/labour costs, land loss to urbanisation, ageing rural rider base |
| **Western Europe** | Olympic disciplines + Paris 2024 halo, exports, equestrian tourism | Cost inflation, animal welfare regulation, social licence pressure |
| **Middle East** | Royal patronage, USD 35 M+ purses (Saudi Cup), women's entry into the sport, ESG-tourism investments | Climate/heat constraints, narrow rider base, dependence on imported expertise |
| **China** | Middle-class luxury spend, government soft-power push, FEI events | Post-2021 cooling, club consolidation, regulatory uncertainty |
| **India** | HNI growth, women's participation, polo heritage, equestrian schools | Land scarcity, no organised supply chain, governance fragmentation, low base |
| **LatAm** | Polo & gaucho traditions, breeding exports | Currency volatility, smaller competitive infrastructure outside Argentina |
| **Australia** | High horse-per-capita, strong leisure base | Geographic dispersion, drought / feed cost risk |

Global cross-cutting trends:
- **Women drive 80–90 % of non-racing participation** (FEI, BETA, FFE, KNHS). Anything a marketplace ships must be designed female-first.
- **Equestrian tourism** is its own USD 2 B+ pool growing 7.2 %/yr.
- **E-commerce** is the channel that wins: +8.3 % vs. -2.1 % offline.

## 7. What This Means for Indusequine

- **You're early on the right curve.** India sits at a USD ~75-140 M equestrian goods market growing 8.4 %/yr — small enough to dominate, large enough to fund a real business. Premium tack is almost entirely imported; there is no organised D2C player.
- **E-commerce is the wedge.** Globally, online tack grows ~8 %/yr while offline declines ~2 %/yr. India's offline infrastructure barely exists for tack — meaning Indusequine doesn't even have to displace anyone, it just has to *exist credibly*.
- **Build female-first.** 80-85 % of riders globally — and emerging-market data points the same way — are women, often young. Brand, photography, product mix, and community should be designed for that audience from day one.
- **Anchor on the four hubs that matter: Bangalore (Embassy), Mumbai (ARC), Delhi-NCR (Army + Delhi Riding Club), Jaipur (polo).** Hyderabad and Pune are tier-2 priorities. Manipur is a cultural / PR play, not a revenue base.
- **Use polo as the heritage story to investors** — India is the birthplace of modern polo. This converts a "small market" pitch into a "global cultural property" pitch. Argentina monetised this; India never has.
- **Geographic expansion order (post-India dominance):** UAE → Saudi Arabia (royal patronage, USD 35 M Saudi Cup, English-speaking) → Southeast Asia (Thailand, Singapore) → eventually compete in Australia / UK with a "Made in India, exported globally" play on saddlery and apparel (mirror the Indian textile-to-global luxury route).
- **The investor headline numbers:** global equestrian equipment USD 12 B in 2024 (Equerry / Co); horse riding equipment alone growing 5.8-7.8 % to 2030 (Mordor / Grand View); equestrian tourism USD 3.95 B by 2033 (DataIntelo); APAC equipment 7.2 % CAGR; India 8.4 % CAGR. The story: "the marketplace for the fastest-growing market segment of a USD 12 B global industry, where no organised player exists."
- **Watch the headwinds:** animal welfare regulation is tightening globally — design vendor onboarding to surface welfare credentials from day one. It's a moat, not a tax.
